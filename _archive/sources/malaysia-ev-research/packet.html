Malaysia EV Research Packet

VoltVision Challenge 2026 - Evidence Compilation

Compiled: 12 August 2026 Sources: 40+ verified sources (web search, industry reports, academic) Tracks covered: T1 (Insurance Pricing), T2 (Customer Solutions), T3 (Ecosystem Resilience)

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Malaysia’s EV market is at an inflection point. Registrations doubled in 2025 (44,813 units, 5.2% TIV penetration) and hit 9.18% TIV in January 2026. However, structural barriers persist: charging infrastructure at 56% of target, insurance premium misalignment, repair ecosystem immaturity, and fuel subsidies dampening switching urgency. The market is transitioning from CBU-import-driven to CKD-localized production, with Chinese OEMs investing heavily.

4. INSURANCE LANDSCAPE

4.1 Current Pricing Reality

  • EV premiums: 10-30% higher than comparable petrol vehicles
  • Battery cost: RM30,000-RM80,000+ (30-50% of vehicle value)
  • Total loss threshold: Lower for EVs due to battery cost concentration
  • Motor insurance GWP: RM24.2 billion (2025), up 4.8% YoY
  • Motor segment loss: RM289.3 million underwriting loss, combined ratio 103%

4.2 PIAM Key Findings

CEO Chua Kim Soon (May 2026): - Battery = 30-50% of vehicle cost - ADAS sensors drive up repair costs (front-end damage cascades) - EV depreciation: 6% gap vs petrol in early years, up to 14% over time - Import parts logistics = cost pressure - Underwriting loss in motor segment while non-motor profitable

EV-specific risks identified: 1. Battery damage from accidents → high replacement cost 2. Flood water intrusion → potential total loss 3. Specialized repair requirements → limited workshop network 4. Parts import dependency → longer claim cycles 5. Steep depreciation → sum insured volatility

4.3 Insurance Products Launched

Insurer Product Key Features
Zurich Malaysia Z-Driver EV Protect RM120 add-on; home charger (RM15k), portable cable (RM2k), public charging (RM20k)
Great Eastern GREAT EV Battery replacement allowance, special perils, wall charger (RM10k)
Various EV Comprehensive Battery as separate component, flood add-on critical

4.4 Critical Gap: Flood Coverage

  • <20% of EV owners have special perils (flood) add-on
  • 95-98% of all vehicles lack flood coverage
  • Flood damage to EV battery = likely total loss
  • Average flood add-on cost: <RM40/month per RM100k sum insured

4.5 Repair Ecosystem

Workshop availability: - Limited EV-certified workshops nationwide - 7 of 10 independent workshops in Klang Valley: decline EV repairs - Need electrical engineers, not just mechanics - PIAM collaborating with JPJ on workshop standards

Technician certification: - PRO-NET/TVETMARA: 37 Level 2 certified e.MAS technicians (Aug 2026) - MRC Malaysia: IMI UK Level 1-3 EV certification - MILI EV Hybrid: First ISO 9001:2015 certified EV training centre

Total loss dynamics: - Battery damage in “wrong area” = may not be repairable - Airbag deployment often triggers total loss assessment - No workshops willing to store fire-risk vehicles - Repair cycle: weeks to months (vs days for petrol cars)

5.2 Current Recycling Capacity

  • Post-treatment: Exported to home countries for metal extraction
  • Licensed AATFs: Only 2 (Car Medic, Jaring Metal Industries)

5.4 Second-Life Applications

  • PV-ESS integration: 30-50% lower capital cost vs new batteries
  • 70-80% lifecycle CO2 reduction from repurposing
  • Petrol station hybrid systems: 45 kWh usable storage per station
  • SOH threshold: 70%+ for repurposing, 40-70% for other uses, <40% for recycling

5.5 Battery Passport (MS 2818)

  • Digital record of battery lifecycle (production → disposal)
  • Tracks: composition, origin, usage history, recyclability
  • ASEAN first - positioned as regional benchmark
  • Supports circular economy and cross-border traceability

6. TRACK-SPECIFIC EVIDENCE

Track 1: EV Insurance Pricing

Challenge: Limited historical claims data for Malaysian EVs Evidence: - PIAM studying China data for pricing proxies (paultan.org, Oct 2025) - Current pricing uses ICE-based models with unknown adequacy - Battery-specific risks (thermal runaway, water intrusion) not yet priced - Geographic risk factors: flood-prone areas need separate consideration - ADAS calibration costs add RM5,000-15,000 per incident

Data gaps: - No Malaysian-specific EV claims frequency/severity data published - Depreciation curves not standardized - Repair cost benchmarks not established

Track 2: Customer-Centric Solutions

Opportunity: 76% of ASEAN-6 respondents plan EV purchase within 5 years (PwC) Evidence: - Zurich Z-Driver EV Protect: RM120 add-on for battery+charger coverage - Great Eastern GREAT EV: inconvenience allowance for battery replacement - Takaful options emerging (Zurich Z-Driver Takaful) - Charging infrastructure integration (Gentari-Zurich collaboration)

Unmet needs: - Flood coverage uptake <20% - Home wallbox coverage not standard - Portable cable theft/loss not widely covered - Cross-border charging (Singapore-Thailand corridor exists)

Track 3: Ecosystem Resilience

Structural constraints identified: 1. Repair costs: 30-50% battery cost concentration 2. Charging gaps: 56% of target achieved, urban-centric 3. Systemic risk: Total loss frequency higher than ICE 4. Supply chain: Import dependency for parts, especially Chinese brands 5. Workforce: EV technician shortage, high-voltage certification required

Coordination needed: - PIAM-JPJ workshop standards collaboration - Unified charging app (PLANMalaysia initiative) - Battery passport implementation - EPR scheme for manufacturer accountability - Fuel subsidy reform to improve EV cost competitiveness

7. SYNTHESIS: KEY CLAIMS FOR MANUSCRIPT

Supported Claims (multi-source verified)

  1. Malaysia’s EV market doubled in 2025 (JPJ data, MAA data, multiple news sources)
  2. Charging infrastructure at 56% of 2025 target (MEVnet, MITI parliament response)
  3. EV insurance premiums 10-30% higher than ICE (BJAK, PIAM, insurers)
  4. Battery = 30-50% of vehicle value (PIAM, insurance guides)
  5. Repair ecosystem immature (PIAM, FMT, workshop surveys)
  6. 870,000 batteries need recycling by 2050 (MARii projections)
  7. Malaysia trails ASEAN peers (Ember, PwC, Business Times)
  8. Fuel subsidies dampen EV switching (Kenanga, Hong Leong Research)

Conflicting/ Uncertain Claims

  1. CBU tax expiry impact: Some say momentum continues (Proton e.MAS 5), others predict slowdown (Apex Securities)
  2. True EV penetration: MAA data excludes Tesla/Zeekr/XPeng; actual may be higher
  3. Battery lifespan: 8-10 years traditional view vs longer for LFP chemistry
  4. Total loss frequency: Anecdotal vs systematic data pending

Research Gaps

  1. No published Malaysian EV claims frequency/severity data
  2. Limited repair cost benchmarking studies
  3. Consumer willingness-to-pay for EV insurance add-ons
  4. Second-life battery economics at scale
  5. Cross-border insurance portability for EVs
